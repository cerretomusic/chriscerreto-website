Biography

Chris Cerreto is a freelance Drummer, Percussionist, Composer, Arranger, Orchestrator, Copyist, and Foley Artist based in New York City. His performance experience spans Broadway national tours — including The Spongebob Musical, Anastasia the Musical, and Mean Girls Musical — as well as cruise ships and regional theater productions across the country.

His compositions have been performed by the James Madison University Wind Symphony, the Holland Youth Symphony, the Shenandoah Youth Symphony, and numerous other ensembles around the world. His music has won awards in competitions including the New Music for Wind Ensemble Competition, the New Music For Young Musicians Composer Competition, and the Holland Symphony Orchestra's Young American Composer's Competition.

He has also arranged for numerous ensembles, resulting in his music being performed in the Macy's Thanksgiving Day Parade, WGI Indoor Percussion Finals, and more.

He is the co-author and ensemble music composer for A Guide for the Modern Percussionist, published through Eastern Hill Music. Most recently, he was commissioned to compose Gaia — a three-movement Marimba Concerto for percussionist Casey Sagolla-Slamp and the Triangle Brass Band, incorporating electronics.

Molotov

🏆 1st Place — New Music for Wind Ensemble Competition

Duration: 8 minutes

Premiered: April 6, 2016 · Harrisonburg, Virginia

The James Madison University Wind Ensemble
Dr. Stephen P. Bolstad, Director

Through biting octatonicism, dense turbulent textures, and a driving polyrhythmic nature, the music embodies the chaotic, explosive traits of a Molotov Cocktail. The juxtaposition of stability and instability through meter builds tension to a solid arrival at the end, reminiscent of rock and roll.

© Chris Cerreto

Remembering Who You Are

🏆 1st Place — New Music For Young Musicians Composer Competition
🥉 3rd Place — Holland Symphony Orchestra Young American Composer's Competition

Duration: 6:30 minutes

Performed by:

The Shenandoah Youth Symphony (2014)
Maria Lorcas, Director

The Holland Youth Symphony (2015)
Richard Piippo, Director

Remembering Who You Are… is meant to convey the idea of realizing one's true self and the emotions expressed during that time in one's life. The music passes through many deep and powerful emotional expressions, eventually leading the listener to a triumphant and relieving finish.

There are times in life when confusion takes hold of the view of one's self and the kind of person they want to be, but in the end all we need to do is just remember who we are…

Concerto for Marimba and Sample Pad

Gaia

Commission · Casey Sagolla-Slamp & the Triangle Brass Band, Raleigh, NC

Gaia juxtaposes acoustic instruments with electronic instruments the same way Mother Earth is currently comprised of Nature and the technological creations of Man. The soloist uses the Sample Pad to trigger pre-set electronic sounds while simultaneously playing the Marimba. The concerto features polyrhythms, polychords, and genre blending (orchestral, electronic, rock, traditional African). Each movement features voice-overs from speeches by Greta Thunberg.

♩Score PreviewMovement 1 · PDF

Movement 1 — Overabundance

Begins with a Greta Thunberg voice-over as the ensemble enters with African rhythms — hinting at the birth of humanity, when early humans were as much a part of nature as the rest of life. The music celebrates the energy of Mother Earth until the electronics cut off the grandest section. The soloist triggers electronic percussion with one hand while playing Marimba with the other, manifesting the struggle between Organic and Inorganic. The ensemble returns triumphant... for now.

♩Score PreviewMovement 2 · PDF

Movement 2 — As the Oceans Grow

Depicts the melting of the polar ice caps and rising water levels. The metallic percussion represents the frozen glaciers; as the movement progresses these elements are slowly overtaken by the flowing of water. The overall density of the music grows and the register of the Solo Marimba rises to punctuate this growth.

Movement 3 — Wrath or Redemption?  (in production)

Will outline the wrath of Mother Earth — extreme weather, forest fires, viral pandemics — and pose the question: will Mankind provoke this wrath, or use its intellect to redeem itself?

Sample Pad

The Roland SPD-SX is recommended, but any Sampling Pad with at least 9 triggers will work. Mounted in front of the marimba with 9 pads triggered by striking with a stick or mallet.

© Chris Cerreto

for Chamber Quintet (2016)

Stress to Breath

Vibraphone · Harp · Piano · Cello · Flute

Scored for Vibraphone, Harp, Piano, Cello, and Flute — what I believe to be the five most beautiful instruments in the world. The piece begins with a Vibraphone solo that gradually develops into a minimalist piano entrance reminiscent of Philip Glass. Layers are added to enhance the music into a peaceful, trancing state.

The middle section takes a darker dissonant turn. This tense gesture is continually answered by a gentle line in the higher register instruments — representing the conversion of Stress to Breath. Instead of letting ourselves be crippled by the overwhelming stress of everyday life, we must use it as breath to keep us moving and alive.

© Chris Cerreto

for Brass Trio and Electronic Playback (2016)

be kind rewind

Trumpet · Horn · Trombone

Scored for Trumpet, Horn, Trombone, and electronic accompaniment, be kind rewind offers a darker take on the combination of EDM and chamber music. By the use of retrogradable rhythms, register play, and tape player samples, the music creates a rewinding, reversal effect.

This is most clearly seen in the second section: A tape player's play button cues a slow, low register Trombone melody. The tape is then pushed into rewind — the melody plays backwards, twice as fast, and 4 octaves higher in the horn and trumpet. This builds into a Dub-Step like conclusion with the use of brass mutes.

© Chris Cerreto

for Percussion Ensemble and SATB Choir (2015)

Omnipresence

Omnipresence for Percussion Ensemble and SATB Choir represents the idea of an all powerful, omnipresent being — or a God. The piece does not subject itself to a specific religion but serves as a "what-if" to the possibility of its existence.

The piece represents everything from this being's rage — with powerful, metallic percussion passages and intense harmonies — to its mercy and benevolence, represented in angelic sounding orchestration between bowed mallet percussion and the beauty of the human voice.

The text is influenced by verses from different religious texts and features primal syllabic chanting. The softer section quotes the Lacrimosa from Verdi's Requiem Mass, chosen in reference to the prisoners in the Theresienstadt concentration camp who performed Verdi's Requiem for the Nazi guards as an act of defiance — reminding us that music and faith are culturally universal.

© Chris Cerreto

Dream(er)

At some point in our lives we have all had our own personal dreams and ambitions — which means we have all experienced our own personal hardships and failures while reaching for them. Dream(er) is an ode to all those who have experienced the ups and downs of following your dreams, taking the listener on an emotional journey through the thoughts and feelings of today's bravest individuals: those who follow their dreams.

© Chris Cerreto

for Percussion Duet (2015)

Furaha

Marimba & Hand Percussion

Furaha means joy, fun in Swahili. The piece is a duet for Marimba and hand percussion, taking these instruments back to their original African and Central American roots. Through dance-like rhythms, quirky harmonies, and playful melodies, Furaha will make listeners bob their heads and smile. The piece involves audience participation and was written to be choreographed alongside dancers.

© Chris Cerreto

for Percussion Ensemble · 7 players (2017)

Visceral

Commissioned by the William G. Enloe Percussion Ensemble, Visceral bases itself off of our raw intuitive human instincts. Through gritty harmonies and heavy ostinatos, the piece musically conveys the intense feeling of a gut reaction. These sensations can vary between our emotions and can shift in a heartbeat, so the music acts accordingly.

Works · Pedagogical Series

Electric Etudes

The Electric Etudes series is a pedagogical series of etudes aimed to enhance the performer's understanding of each specific instrument while mastering its most useful elements. Each instrument features two levels of difficulty. All synths were created from scratch — no presets were used.

Triangle Level 1 — Sync

The performer plays along with a mixed-meter electronic playback while developing the use of triangle rolls and different textures. The focus is to "sync" these rhythms perfectly with the electronics.

Triangle Level 2 — Meta

The electronic playback was created by sampling the three triangles used in the original recording. This etude features a wide variety of extended techniques, beginning with 4-mallet technique and progressing through multiple sections.

Tambourine Level 1 — Ease

Focuses on multiple essential techniques for the tambourine. The performer's focus should be to maintain accuracy and sound quality while being as relaxed — or at "ease" — as possible.

More etudes coming soon...

© Chris Cerreto

It begins with an atmospheric extra-terrestrial theme and quickly decays into a supernova. The ending features an air vacuum effect, followed by the preceding music played in reverse until the end.

Publication · Eastern Hill Music

A Guide For The Modern Percussionist

Buy the Book ↗

Co-authored by Casey D'Sola and Chris Cerreto

This essential percussion education resource covers grades 6–12 in areas including snare drum, 2+4 mallets, timpani, tambourine, triangle, cymbals, bass drum, and more. It is one of the last books any school will need to buy for their program.

The book also includes 9 percussion ensemble pieces progressing through each skill level. The pieces have varying numbers of players and equipment required so that it is equally accessible for small and large programs. Scores and parts are accessible virtually.

This book is broken into three resources: Method Book, Ensemble Scores, and Video Lessons.
